15. Shifted QR Iteration

Numerical Methods I — Florian Schaefer. Source: Lecture 15.pdf, pp. 1–7.

15.1 Recap: Hessenberg QR and Bulge Chasing

The preceding lecture reduced \(A\) to an upper Hessenberg matrix

\[ H=Q^*AQ. \]

For Hessenberg \(T_{k-1}\), a QR step uses adjacent Givens rotations:

\[ T_{k-1}=U_kR_k, \qquad R_k=G_{m-1}^*\cdots G_1^*T_{k-1}, \qquad T_k=R_kG_1\cdots G_{m-1}=R_kU_k. \]

The interleaved similarity \(G_1^*T_{k-1}G_1\) creates a small bulge below the Hessenberg band. Subsequent paired rotations chase it down the matrix until Hessenberg form is restored. The implicit \(Q\) theorem explains why the first column of the orthogonal factor determines this process.

15.2 Proof of the Implicit \(Q\) Theorem

Suppose \(Q\) and \(V\) are orthogonal and

\[ Q^*AQ=H, \qquad V^*AV=G, \]

where \(H\) and \(G\) are unreduced upper Hessenberg. Choose the same first column, \(q_1=v_1\). We show successively that \(q_i=\pm v_i\) for \(i=2,\ldots,m\) in the real case. Set

\[ W=V^*Q. \]

This is orthogonal, and its first column is \(e_1\). From \(AQ=QH\) and \(AV=VG\),

\[ GW=V^*AVV^*Q=V^*AQ=V^*QH=WH. \]

Suppose the first \(i\) columns of \(W\) are \(\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_i\). Column \(i\) of \(WH\) has the form

\[ (WH)_{:,i} =\sum_{j=1}^{i+1}H_{ji}w_j, \]

because \(H\) is upper Hessenberg. Since \(H_{i+1,i}\ne0\), this equation determines the next column:

\[ H_{i+1,i}w_{i+1}=Gw_i-\sum_{j=1}^{i}H_{ji}w_j. \]

The known columns lie in \(\operatorname{span}(e_1,\ldots,e_i)\), and \(G e_i\in\operatorname{span}(e_1,\ldots,e_{i+1})\). Thus \(w_{i+1}\) also lies in the first \(i+1\) coordinate directions. Orthogonality to the preceding columns leaves only

\[ w_{i+1}=\pm e_{i+1}. \]

By induction \(W\) is diagonal with signs on its diagonal, so the remaining columns of \(Q=VW\) are fixed up to signs. (For complex unitary matrices, replace signs by unit-modulus phases.)

15.3 Shifted QR Iteration

An ordinary QR step factors and reverses

\[ T_{k-1}=U_kR_k, \qquad T_k=R_kU_k. \]

For a shifted step, select \(\mu_k\) and instead compute

\[ T_{k-1}-\mu_kI=U_kR_k, \qquad T_k=R_kU_k+\mu_kI. \]

The shift preserves similarity, since

\[ T_k =U_k^*(T_{k-1}-\mu_kI)U_k+\mu_kI =U_k^*T_{k-1}U_k. \]

It therefore preserves the eigenvalues of the original matrix. The accumulated orthogonal factors can be tracked as in ordinary QR iteration. The purpose of the shift is to speed convergence of selected eigenvalues.

15.4 Choosing a Shift and Deflation

Suppose \(\mu_k\) is an eigenvalue of the unreduced upper-Hessenberg matrix \(T_{k-1}\). Then \(T_{k-1}-\mu_kI\) is singular. Its first \(m-1\) columns are independent: their submatrix on rows \(2,\ldots,m\) is upper triangular, with the nonzero subdiagonal entries of \(T_{k-1}\) on its diagonal. Its QR factorization therefore has a zero last diagonal entry in \(R_k\). Consequently \(R_kU_k+\mu_kI\) has a zero last subdiagonal entry: the last eigenvalue has deflated into a \(1\times1\) block.

To understand the intended choice, imagine \(T_{k-1}\) close to upper triangular, with diagonal entries \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m\). A shift near \(\lambda_m\) makes \(\lambda_m-\mu_k\) small compared with the other \(\lambda_i-\mu_k\); this favors rapid convergence of the bottom-right entry. The lecture proposes \(\mu_k\approx\lambda_m\).

In the Hessenberg picture, \(T_{k-1}-\mu_kI\) retains its structure, and a QR step drives the last subdiagonal entry toward zero. Once \((T_k)_{m,m-1}\) is sufficiently small, split off the last row and column and continue on the leading \((m-1)\times(m-1)\) block. Repeating this deflation process reveals the eigenvalues along the diagonal.

The source’s structural diagrams depict the singular shifted factorization and the isolated last eigenvalue:

\[ T_{k-1}-\mu_kI=U_kR_k, \qquad (R_k)_{mm}=0, \qquad T_k=R_kU_k+\mu_kI =\begin{pmatrix} (T_k)_{11}&(T_k)_{12}\\ 0&\mu_k \end{pmatrix}, \]

where the block sizes are \((m-1)\) and \(1\). After exact deflation, the last eigenvalue needs no further iteration.

15.5 A Practical QR Iteration

The lecture’s practical outline is:

  1. Reduce \(A\) to upper Hessenberg form \(H=Q^*AQ\).
  2. Until convergence, choose a shift \(\mu_k\) near an eigenvalue, factor \(T_{k-1}-\mu_kI=U_kR_k\), and set \(T_k=R_kU_k+\mu_kI\).
  3. If a subdiagonal entry becomes negligible, split the matrix into diagonal blocks. Apply the same process recursively to the remaining blocks.

The shift choice aims to make one subdiagonal entry small quickly. The lecture emphasizes \(\mu_k\approx\lambda_m\): under the shifted spectrum, \(|\lambda_m-\mu_k|\ll|\lambda_i-\mu_k|\) for the other eigenvalues. Choosing a small eigenvalue is easiest when the eigenvalues are already ordered appropriately; reordering can slow convergence. The resulting block upper-triangular matrix has the eigenvalues of its diagonal blocks; a converged \(1\times1\) block supplies one eigenvalue.

15.6 Relation to Shifted Orthogonal and Inverse Iteration

For ordinary QR iteration with \(Q_0=I\), the accumulated QR factors agree with orthogonal iteration. With shifts, the same relation holds for the shifted factors. For the first two steps,

\[ \begin{aligned} H-\mu_1I&=U_1R_1,\\ T_1-\mu_2I&=U_2R_2,\\ U_1U_2R_2R_1 &=U_1(T_1-\mu_2I)R_1\\ &=U_1(R_1U_1+\mu_1I-\mu_2I)R_1\\ &=(H-\mu_1I)(H-\mu_2I)\\ &=(H-\mu_2I)(H-\mu_1I). \end{aligned} \]

Thus \(U_1U_2\) is the orthogonal factor obtained by factoring the product of shifted matrices (with the indicated right-to-left order). Continuing gives the general identity

\[ (U_1\cdots U_k)(R_k\cdots R_1) =(H-\mu_kI)\cdots(H-\mu_1I) =\widehat Q_k\widehat R_k. \]

This is shifted orthogonal power iteration, with \(\widehat Q_k=U_1\cdots U_k\). If each shifted matrix is invertible, applying the inverse adjoint to the same factorization gives

\[ \widehat Q_k\widehat R_k^{-*} =\bigl[(H-\mu_kI)\cdots(H-\mu_1I)\bigr]^{-*} =(H-\mu_kI)^{-*}\cdots(H-\mu_1I)^{-*}. \]

The first columns of \(\widehat Q_k\) follow the shifted power iteration, while its last columns can be understood through this inverse-adjoint product. As the lecture concludes, QR iteration combines power and inverse iteration in one process.

15.7 Corrections and Clarifications to the Source

  • Page 2: The proof spells out the induction implicit in the handwritten argument. In the complex case, the ambiguity is a phase rather than a sign.
  • Pages 4–5: The exact-eigenvalue argument assumes the Hessenberg matrix is unreduced. This ensures the first \(m-1\) shifted columns are independent, so singularity appears in the last QR diagonal entry.
  • Page 7: Wrote the general shifted-product and inverse-adjoint identities following the source, with invertibility stated for the inverse-adjoint expression.
