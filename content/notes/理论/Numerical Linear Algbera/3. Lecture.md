## 3.2 LU Factorization

We write 
$$
A=(L_{m}\cdots L_{1})^{-1} U = LU
$$

Compute by transforming $A$ into $U$, storing $L_{i}$. We can overwrite $A$ with $L$ and $U$.

### 3.2.1 Algorithm of LU Factorization

%%
For k = 1 =m
	A[(k+1):m,k] / = A [k, k]
	 For i = (k+1) : m
	 	 For i = (k+1) : m
		 	 A[i, j] -= A [i, k] A[k, i]
		End
	End
End

> **Does $LU$ algorithm  nun to completion for all invertible?**

No ! Consider $A = (0,1;1,1)$ Division by 0. $A[1,1]$ is a zero point.


> [!theorem]
> If the $k$-th pivot of $LU$ factorization is zero, then $A[1:k,1:k]$ is singular. $LU$ has nonzero pivots iff all leading sub-matrices are nonsingular.

Proof: 
$$
\begin{align}
A = LU   \Longrightarrow &  A[1:k,1:k]\\
=  & L[1:k,:]U[:,1:k] \\
=  & \underbrace{L[1:k,1:k]}_{\text{Nonsingular}}
   \underbrace{U[1:k,1:k]}_{
   \substack{
   \text{Nonsingular iff first } k \\
   \text{pivots are nonsingular}
   }}
\end{align}
$$
