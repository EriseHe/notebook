## 4.1 Recap

<!-- Source: Lecture 04.pdf, p. 1. -->

Gaussian elimination applies elementary lower-triangular matrices to obtain

$$
\underbrace{L_m\cdots L_1A}_{U}x=L_m\cdots L_1b
$$

Since the inverses of the elementary matrices are lower triangular,

$$
A=LU,
\qquad
L=L_1^{-1}\cdots L_m^{-1}
$$

The upper triangle of the overwritten array stores $U$, and its strict lower triangle stores the multipliers of $L$. A zero pivot can stop elimination even for an invertible matrix. The small-pivot example

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\epsilon^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&1\\0&1-\epsilon^{-1}\end{pmatrix}
$$

also shows why nonzero but very small pivots can cause loss of accuracy.

## 4.2 Error Analysis for LU Factorization

<!-- Source: Lecture 04.pdf, pp. 2–3. -->

Here $\epsilon$ is machine precision, and $L,U$ denote the computed factors. Absolute values and matrix inequalities are interpreted entrywise.

**Theorem — first-order form.** Suppose LU factorization terminates without a zero pivot and the operations satisfy the relative rounding-error model. Then

$$
A=LU+E,
\qquad
|E|\lesssim m\epsilon\,|L|\,|U|
$$

The product on the right is ordinary matrix multiplication:

$$
(|L|\,|U|)_{ik}=\sum_{j=1}^m |L_{ij}|\,|U_{jk}|
$$

### 4.2.1 Entrywise Relations

In exact arithmetic, triangularity gives $A_{ik}=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(i,k)}L_{ij}U_{jk}$. If $i\le k$, isolate the term with $j=i$ and use $L_{ii}=1$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ik}
&=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}+\underbrace{L_{ii}}_{1}U_{ik}\\
U_{ik}
&=A_{ik}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}
\end{aligned}
$$

If $i>k$, isolate the term with $j=k$ and divide by the nonzero pivot $U_{kk}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ik}
&=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}+L_{ik}U_{kk}\\
L_{ik}
&=\frac{A_{ik}-\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}}{U_{kk}}
\end{aligned}
$$

### 4.2.2 Floating-Point Summation

The lecture uses the dot-product estimate from HW2, to first order:

$$
\operatorname{fl}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^m x_i y_i\right)
=\sum_{i=1}^m x_i y_i(1+\delta_i),
\qquad
|\delta_i|\lesssim m\epsilon
$$

Here $\operatorname{fl}$ indicates floating-point evaluation.

### 4.2.3 Error in an Entry of U

For $i\le k$, the computed entry satisfies

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{ik}
&=\left(A_{ik}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}(1+\delta_j')\right)(1+\delta')\\
|\delta_j'|&\lesssim(i-1)\epsilon,
\qquad |\delta'|\le\epsilon
\end{aligned}
$$

To collect all rounding terms into one error sum, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta_j&=\delta_j',\qquad j<i\\
\delta_i&=\frac{1}{1+\delta'}-1
=-\frac{\delta'}{1+\delta'}\\
|\delta_i|&\le\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\lesssim\epsilon
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus every coefficient satisfies $|\delta_j|\lesssim m\epsilon$ to first order. Rearranging the computed-entry formula and then bounding its error gives one chain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ik}
&=\frac{L_{ii}U_{ik}}{1+\delta'}
  +\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}(1+\delta_j')\\
&=L_{ii}U_{ik}(1+\delta_i)
  +\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}(1+\delta_j)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{i}L_{ij}U_{jk}
  +\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{i}L_{ij}U_{jk}\delta_j}_{E_{ik}}\\
|E_{ik}|
&\le\sum_{j=1}^{i}|L_{ij}|\,|U_{jk}|\,|\delta_j|\\
&\lesssim m\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^{i}|L_{ij}|\,|U_{jk}|\\
&=m\epsilon\,(|L|\,|U|)_{ik}
\end{aligned}
$$

The last equality holds because $L_{ij}=0$ for $j>i$.

### 4.2.4 Error in an Entry of L

For $i>k$, division by $U_{kk}$ introduces one more rounding factor:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{ik}
&=(1+\delta'')
\frac{(1+\delta')\left(A_{ik}-\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}(1+\delta_j')\right)}{U_{kk}}\\
|\delta_j'|&\lesssim(k-1)\epsilon,
\qquad |\delta'|,|\delta''|\le\epsilon
\end{aligned}
$$

As in the preceding case, absorb the reciprocal rounding factors into the coefficient of the last term:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta_j&=\delta_j',\qquad j<k\\
\delta_k&=\frac{1}{(1+\delta')(1+\delta'')}-1\\
&=-\frac{\delta'+\delta''+\delta'\delta''}
{(1+\delta')(1+\delta'')}\\
|\delta_k|&\le\frac{2\epsilon+\epsilon^2}{(1-\epsilon)^2}
\lesssim2\epsilon\le m\epsilon
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $|1+\delta'|,|1+\delta''|\ge1-\epsilon$, and $m\ge2$ because $i>k$. Therefore $|\delta_j|\lesssim m\epsilon$ for every coefficient in this case as well. Now

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ik}
&=\frac{L_{ik}U_{kk}}{(1+\delta')(1+\delta'')}
  +\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}(1+\delta_j')\\
&=L_{ik}U_{kk}(1+\delta_k)
  +\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}L_{ij}U_{jk}(1+\delta_j)\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{k}L_{ij}U_{jk}
  +\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{k}L_{ij}U_{jk}\delta_j}_{E_{ik}}\\
|E_{ik}|
&\le\sum_{j=1}^{k}|L_{ij}|\,|U_{jk}|\,|\delta_j|\\
&\lesssim m\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^{k}|L_{ij}|\,|U_{jk}|\\
&=m\epsilon\,(|L|\,|U|)_{ik}
\end{aligned}
$$

Terms with $j>k$ vanish because $U_{jk}=0$. Both cases therefore give the componentwise estimate in the theorem. Large intermediate entries in $L$ and $U$ can make this bound large.

## 4.3 Pivots

<!-- Source: Lecture 04.pdf, p. 4. -->

Before step $k$, the packed array has the form

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
U_{11}&\cdots&U_{1,k-1}&U_{1k}&U_{1,k+1}&\cdots&U_{1m}\\
L_{21}&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&U_{k-1,k-1}&U_{k-1,k}&U_{k-1,k+1}&\cdots&U_{k-1,m}\\
L_{k1}&\cdots&L_{k,k-1}&\widetilde A_{kk}&\widetilde A_{k,k+1}&\cdots&\widetilde A_{km}\\
L_{k+1,1}&\cdots&L_{k+1,k-1}&\widetilde A_{k+1,k}&\widetilde A_{k+1,k+1}&\cdots&\widetilde A_{k+1,m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
L_{m1}&\cdots&L_{m,k-1}&\widetilde A_{mk}&\widetilde A_{m,k+1}&\cdots&\widetilde A_{mm}
\end{pmatrix}
$$

The tildes denote the current active entries. After the step, the array has the form

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
U_{11}&\cdots&U_{1,k-1}&U_{1k}&U_{1,k+1}&\cdots&U_{1m}\\
L_{21}&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&U_{k-1,k-1}&U_{k-1,k}&U_{k-1,k+1}&\cdots&U_{k-1,m}\\
L_{k1}&\cdots&L_{k,k-1}&U_{kk}&U_{k,k+1}&\cdots&U_{km}\\
L_{k+1,1}&\cdots&L_{k+1,k-1}&L_{k+1,k}&\ast&\cdots&\ast\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
L_{m1}&\cdots&L_{m,k-1}&L_{mk}&\ast&\cdots&\ast
\end{pmatrix}
$$

The stars denote the updated trailing block. The entries below the pivot store multipliers rather than the zeros of the mathematical elimination matrix.

The entry $\widetilde A_{kk}$ is the **pivot**. The new multipliers, pivot row, and products in the trailing update satisfy

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{ik}&=\frac{\widetilde A_{ik}}{\widetilde A_{kk}},\qquad i>k\\
U_{kj}&=\widetilde A_{kj},\qquad j\ge k\\
L_{ik}U_{kj}
&=\frac{\widetilde A_{ik}\widetilde A_{kj}}{\widetilde A_{kk}}\\
\max_{i,j>k}|L_{ik}U_{kj}|
&=\frac{\displaystyle\max_{i,j>k}|\widetilde A_{ik}\widetilde A_{kj}|}
{|\widetilde A_{kk}|}
\end{aligned}
$$

Each star in the trailing block is the updated value $\widetilde A_{ij}-L_{ik}U_{kj}$. A small denominator can make the subtracted product large.

We need to avoid zero pivots and would also like to avoid small pivots.

## 4.4 How to Pivot: Row Pivoting

<!-- Source: Lecture 04.pdf, p. 5. -->

Choose the largest-magnitude entry in the active part of column $k$:

$$
\kappa\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{i=k,\ldots,m}|\widetilde A_{ik}|
$$

Let $P_k$ swap rows $k$ and $\kappa$. On the active rows and columns, the matrix picture is

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
\widetilde A_{kk}&\cdots&\widetilde A_{km}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
\widetilde A_{\kappa k}&\cdots&\widetilde A_{\kappa m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots
\end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\ P_k\ }
\begin{pmatrix}
\widetilde A_{\kappa k}&\cdots&\widetilde A_{\kappa m}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
\widetilde A_{kk}&\cdots&\widetilde A_{km}\\
\vdots&&\vdots
\end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\ L_k\ }
\begin{pmatrix}
\widetilde A_{\kappa k}&\cdots&\widetilde A_{\kappa m}\\
0&\ast&\ast\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&\ast&\ast
\end{pmatrix}
$$

The row swap places the chosen entry on the diagonal; elimination then zeros the entries below it. If the chosen pivot is nonzero,

$$
\begin{aligned}
|(P_k\widetilde A)_{kk}|
&=|\widetilde A_{\kappa k}|
=\max_{r=k,\ldots,m}|\widetilde A_{rk}|\\
|(P_k\widetilde A)_{ik}|
&\le\max_{r=k,\ldots,m}|\widetilde A_{rk}|
=|(P_k\widetilde A)_{kk}|\\
|L_{ik}|
&=\frac{|(P_k\widetilde A)_{ik}|}{|(P_k\widetilde A)_{kk}|}
\le1,\qquad i>k
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus row pivoting bounds the multipliers. It does not by itself bound all entries of $U$.

## 4.5 Commuting Permutations

<!-- Source: Lecture 04.pdf, p. 6. -->

With row swaps interleaved with elimination,

$$
U=L_mP_m\cdots L_1P_1A
$$

Let $P_\ell$ be a later row swap, so $\ell>k$. Since $P_\ell^2=I$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_\ell L_k
&=P_\ell L_k\underbrace{P_\ell P_\ell}_{I}\\
&=\underbrace{(P_\ell L_kP_\ell)}_{\text{swap rows and columns of }L_k}P_\ell
\end{aligned}
$$

The row and column swaps exchange two entries below the diagonal in column $k$. Write these entries as $\delta_\ell$ and $\delta_i$, where $k<\ell<i$. On rows and columns $(k,\ell,i)$, the two operations are

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
\delta_\ell&1&0\\
\delta_i&0&1
\end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\ \text{swap rows }\ell,i\ }
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
\delta_i&0&1\\
\delta_\ell&1&0
\end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\ \text{swap columns }\ell,i\ }
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
\delta_i&1&0\\
\delta_\ell&0&1
\end{pmatrix}
$$

The other multiplier entries are unchanged. Since the later swap acts only on indices greater than $k$, it leaves $e_k$ fixed. The rank-one form from Lecture 3 gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_\ell L_kP_\ell
&=P_\ell(I-\ell_ke_k^*)P_\ell\\
&=P_\ell^2-(P_\ell\ell_k)(e_k^*P_\ell)\\
&=I-(P_\ell\ell_k)e_k^*
\end{aligned}
$$

The first $k$ entries of $P_\ell\ell_k$ are still zero, so this is again elementary lower triangular. For the first two elimination steps, the rearrangement reads

$$
L_2P_2L_1P_1A
=L_2(P_2L_1P_2)(P_2P_1)A
$$

Repeating this operation moves all permutations to the right. The tildes below denote the elementary factors after their multipliers have been reordered:

$$
\begin{aligned}
U
&=\widetilde L_m\cdots\widetilde L_1
\underbrace{P_m\cdots P_1}_{P}A\\
PA
&=(\widetilde L_m\cdots\widetilde L_1)^{-1}U\\
&=\underbrace{\widetilde L_1^{-1}\cdots\widetilde L_m^{-1}}_{L}U\\
&=LU
\end{aligned}
$$

The inverse factors are unit lower triangular, so their product $L$ is unit lower triangular as well.

## 4.6 Updating the Packed Factorization

<!-- Source: Lecture 04.pdf, p. 7. -->

Suppose the current elimination state satisfies

$$
L_k\cdots L_1PA=U_k
$$

For the next row swap $P_{k+1}$, define

$$
L_j'=P_{k+1}L_jP_{k+1},\quad j=1,\ldots,k,
\qquad P'=P_{k+1}P
$$

The new elimination state follows by inserting $P_{k+1}^2=I$ between the earlier factors:

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{k+1}
&=L_{k+1}P_{k+1}U_k\\
&=L_{k+1}P_{k+1}L_k\cdots L_1PA\\
&=L_{k+1}(P_{k+1}L_kP_{k+1})\cdots
(P_{k+1}L_1P_{k+1})P_{k+1}PA\\
&=L_{k+1}L_k'\cdots L_1'P'A
\end{aligned}
$$

Conjugating the earlier elementary factors reorders the multipliers already stored in the first $k$ columns. Therefore **swap the complete rows of the packed array**, including those earlier columns. Also swap the corresponding rows of $P$.

## 4.7 Algorithm: LU Factorization with Row Pivoting

<!-- Source: Lecture 04.pdf, p. 8. -->

The lecture's pseudocode, in Julia notation, assumes each selected pivot is nonzero. The index returned by `argmax` is relative to `A[k:m, k]`, so adding `k - 1` converts it to a row index of $A$:

```julia
using LinearAlgebra

m = size(A, 1)
P = Matrix{Int}(I, m, m)

for k in 1:m
    κ = k - 1 + argmax(abs.(A[k:m, k]))

    for j in 1:m
        A[κ, j], A[k, j] = A[k, j], A[κ, j]
        P[κ, j], P[k, j] = P[k, j], P[κ, j]
    end

    for i in (k + 1):m
        A[i, k] /= A[k, k]
    end

    for i in (k + 1):m
        for j in (k + 1):m
            A[i, j] -= A[i, k] * A[k, j]
        end
    end
end
```

The strict lower triangle stores $L$ without its unit diagonal; the diagonal and upper triangle store $U$. Relative to the original, unmodified input,

$$
P A_{\mathrm{original}}=LU
$$
