      2. Transport equation

      2026-09-09 · PDE · transport-equation

      2.1 Evans §2.1 — Transport equation

      Let \(b\in\mathbb R^n\) be a constant velocity and let \(g:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) be the initial data:

      \[ \begin{cases} u_t+b\cdot Du=f(x,t), & (x,t)\in\mathbb R^n\times(0,\infty),\\ u(x,0)=g(x). & \end{cases} \]

      The operator \(\partial_t+b\cdot D\) differentiates in the space-time direction \((b,1)\).

      2.1.1 Characteristics

      To find the value at a fixed endpoint \((x,t)\), trace its characteristic backward. Let

      \[ X(\tau)=x-(t-\tau)b,\qquad 0\leq \tau\leq t. \]

      Then \(X'(\tau)=b\), \(X(t)=x\), and the characteristic meets the initial plane at

      \[ X(0)=x-tb. \]

      Along this curve,

      \[ \frac{d}{d\tau}u(X(\tau),\tau) =Du(X(\tau),\tau)\cdot X'(\tau)+u_t(X(\tau),\tau) =b\cdot Du+u_t =f(X(\tau),\tau). \]

      Integrating from \(0\) to \(t\) gives the characteristic formula

      \[ \boxed{ u(x,t)=g(x-tb)+\int_0^t f\bigl(x-(t-\tau)b,\tau\bigr)\,d\tau. } \]

      Equivalently, the integrand may be written as \(f(x+(\tau-t)b,\tau)\).

      2.1.1.1 Homogeneous equation

      If \(f=0\), the value of \(u\) is constant along each characteristic:

      \[ \boxed{u(x,t)=g(x-tb).} \]

      Thus the initial profile is translated with velocity \(b\) without changing shape. The characteristic lines satisfy

      \[ x-tb=\text{constant}. \]

      2.1.2 Slides 4–5 correction

      CorrectionDo not use the formula shown incorrectly in the recording

      Wrong: \(z(s)=u(x+sbt,t+s)\)
      Correct: \(z(s)=u(x+sb,t+s)\).

      For the correct path, with \(s\in[-t,0]\),

      \[ z'(s)=b\cdot Du(x+sb,t+s)+u_t(x+sb,t+s). \]

      Also,

      \[ z(0)=u(x,t),\qquad z(-t)=u(x-tb,0)=g(x-tb). \]

      Hence

      \[ u(x,t)-g(x-tb) =\int_{-t}^{0}f(x+sb,t+s)\,ds, \]

      which becomes the boxed formula after setting \(\tau=t+s\).

      The erroneous \(x+sbt\) has derivative \(tb\) with respect to \(s\), so it produces \(z'(s)=tb\cdot Du+u_t\), not the transport operator \(b\cdot Du+u_t\); at \(s=-t\) it also lands at the wrong point \(x-t^2b\).

      2.1.3 Quick checks and regularity

      • For \(f=0\), \(u_t=-b\cdot Dg(x-tb)\) and \(Du=Dg(x-tb)\), so \(u_t+b\cdot Du=0\).
      • If \(g\in C^1\) (and \(f\) is sufficiently regular), the formula gives a classical solution.
      • For rougher, locally integrable data, the same formula is interpreted distributionally as a weak solution; lack of smoothness is transported rather than smoothed out.
      • Memory aid: to compute \(u(x,t)\), go backward by \(tb\) to the footpoint \(x-tb\), then accumulate the source \(f\) forward along that line.
