      2.2 Finite Difference

      2.2 Finite Differences

      Two mathematically equivalent strong formulations may be different numerically:

      \[ -\Delta u=f. \]

      On the other hand, we can write

      \[ -\nabla\cdot(\nabla u)=f \Longrightarrow \left\{ \begin{aligned} \nabla u &= \sigma \\ \nabla\cdot\sigma &= f. \end{aligned} \right. \]

      2.2.1 Poisson Equation in 1D

      \[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} -u''&=f,\qquad x\in[0,1] \\ u(0)&=u(1)=0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

      • Max-Min Principle

      • Discretization:

        \[ -u''(x_j)=f(x_j). \]

        Collocation/Mesh. \(|h|\) is the step size.

      Claim

      Claim

      \[ (u')'\approx \frac{u(x_{j+1})-2u(x_j)+u(x_{j-1})}{h^2}. \]

      Proof 1. Denote \(u(x_{j+1})=u_{j+1}\). By Taylor expansion:

      \[ \begin{aligned} u_{j+1} &=u_j+u'(x_j)h+\frac{1}{2}u''(x_j)h^2+\frac{1}{3!}u'''(x_j)h^3+\frac{1}{4!}u^{(4)}(x_j)h^4+O(h^5) \\ u_{j-1} &=u_j-u'(x_j)h+\frac{1}{2}u''(x_j)h^2-\frac{1}{3!}u'''(x_j)h^3+\frac{1}{4!}u^{(4)}(x_j)h^4+O(h^5) \\ u_{j+1}+u_{j-1} &=2u_j+u''(x_j)h^2+\frac{2}{4!}u^{(4)}h^4+O(h^6) \\ u''(x_j) &=\frac{u_{j+1}+u_{j-1}-2u_j}{h^2} +\underbrace{\frac{1}{12}u^{(4)}(x_j)h^2}_{\tau(h),\ \text{consistency error}}. \end{aligned} \]

      Hence, when we make the mesh finer \((h\to \frac{1}{2}h)\), the consistency error decrease by 4 times (order of 2).
      Q.E.D. \(\blacksquare\)

      • Form the numerical problem: Except for the endpoints, we can write

        \[ -\frac{u_{j+1}-2u_j+u_{j-1}}{h^2}=f_j. \]

      Denote

      \[ u= \begin{bmatrix} u_1\\ u_2\\ \vdots\\ u_{N-1} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad f= \begin{bmatrix} f_1\\ f_2\\ \vdots\\ f_{N-1} \end{bmatrix}. \]

      Define

      \[ A=\frac{1}{h^2} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & \ddots & \ddots \\ 0 & \ddots & \ddots & -1 \\ & & -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}. \]

      Then, we get \(Au=f\).

      Proposition

      Proposition \(A\) is SPD (symmetric positive definite) \(\Longrightarrow\) all eigenvalues of \(A\) are positive.

      Proof 2. We will show \(A\) is symmetric, and \(A\) is positive definite.

      1. Symmetry is trivial.

      2. Positivity: \([\mathrm{WTS}:\ \forall\, x\ne 0,\ x^\top Ax>0.]\)

      \[ \begin{aligned} x^\top Ax &=\frac{1}{h^2}\big(2x_1^2-2x_1x_2+x_2^2+\cdots\big) \\ &=\frac{1}{h^2}\big(x_1^2+(x_1-x_2)^2+\cdots+(x_j-x_{j+1})^2+\cdots+x_{N-1}^2\big) \\ &\ge 0. \end{aligned} \]

      If we have a constant vector:

      \[ x= \begin{bmatrix} x_* \cdots x_* \end{bmatrix}^{\top}. \]

      Then,

      \[ x^\top Ax=\frac{1}{2}(x_*^2+x_*^2)=0 \Longleftrightarrow x_*=0. \]

      Hence, \(\forall\, x\ne 0,\ x^\top Ax=0\).

      So, \(A\) is SPD.
      Q.E.D. \(\blacksquare\)

      Corollary

      Corollary \(A\) is non-singular, and the numerical problem is well-posed.

      • Error Analysis:

      \[ \begin{aligned} u_{\mathrm{num}}&=A^{-1}f \ \Longrightarrow\ \|u_{\mathrm{num}}\|\le \|A^{-1}\|\,\|f\| \\ Au_{\mathrm{ex}}&=f+\tau, \qquad \begin{bmatrix}\tau_j\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} c\,f''(x_j)h^2 \end{bmatrix} \\ e&=u_{\mathrm{ex}}-u_{\mathrm{num}}=A^{-1}\tau \\ \|e\|&\le \|A^{-1}\|\,\underbrace{\|\tau\|}_{O(h^2)}. \end{aligned} \]

      But this is not enough: \(A^{-1}\) also depends on \(h\).

      Proposition

      Proposition \(\|A^{-1}\|_2\ \perp\!\!\!\perp\ h\).

      Proof 3. Recall: \(\|A\|_2=\rho(A)=\max_i|\lambda_i|\), the spectral radius. Then,

      \[ \|A^{-1}\|_2=\frac{1}{\min_i|\lambda_i|}=\rho(A^{-1}). \]

      \([\mathrm{WTS}:\ \text{eigenvalues of }A\ \text{(or }A^{-1}\text{) are independent of }h].\)

      \[ A=\frac{1}{h^2}\,\mathrm{trid}(-1,2,-1), \qquad T=\mathrm{trid}(-1,2,-1). \]

      Solve \(Tx=\lambda x\):

      \[ (T-\lambda I)x=0 \]

      \[ x_{j-1}-(2-\lambda)x_j+x_{j+1}=0. \]

      Characteristic equation:

      \[ 1-(2-\lambda)\rho^2+\rho^2=0 \]

      \[ x_j=a\rho_1^j+b\rho_2^j \qquad\text{or}\qquad x_j=a\rho^j+jb\rho^j. \]

      Imposing \(\Delta\ge 0\), we get \(\lambda\ge 4\). Imposing BC: \(x_0=x_N=0\), we get \(a=b=0\), and thus \(x=0\). Trivial.

      Hence, we don’t have eigenvalues \(\ge 4\), and we should only consider complex solutions.

      \[ \rho_{1,2}=\frac{2-\lambda\pm i\sqrt{2}\sqrt{4-\lambda}}{2} =\cos(\theta)\pm i\sin(\theta) \qquad [\text{Enforcing }|\rho|=1] \]

      So,

      \[ x_j=a\rho_1^j+b\rho_2^j. \]

      Since \(x_0=x_N=0\):

      \[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} a+b&=0 \\ a\rho_1^N+B\rho_2^N&=0 \end{aligned} \right. \Longrightarrow \rho_1^N-\bar{\rho}_1^N=0. \]

      \[ \cos(N\theta)+i\sin(N\theta)-\cos(N\theta)+i\sin(N\theta)=0 \Longrightarrow \theta=\frac{j\pi}{N},\qquad j=1,\ldots,N-1. \]

      Going back, we have

      \[ \begin{aligned} \lambda_j &=-\frac{1}{\rho}+\rho+2 =2\left(1-\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{N}\right)\right) \\ \cos(2\alpha)&=1-2\sin^2(\alpha) \\ 2\sin^2(\alpha)&=1-\cos(2\alpha) \Longrightarrow \lambda_j=4\sin^2\left(\frac{j\pi}{2N}\right). \end{aligned} \]

      Hence, eigenvalues of \(A\):

      \[ \lambda=\frac{4}{h^2}\sin^2\left(\frac{\pi}{2N}\right) =\frac{4}{h^2}\sin^2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}h\right) \qquad [h=\frac{1}{N}] \]

      When \(h\to 0\), \(\sin^2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}h\right)\to \left(\frac{\pi}{2}h\right)^2\). So,

      \[ \lambda=\frac{4}{h^2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^2h^2=\pi^2\ \perp\!\!\!\perp\ h. \]

      Q.E.D. \(\blacksquare\)

      Corollary

      Corollary Our numerical method is convergent.

      \[ e=A^{-1}\tau. \]

      When \(h\to 0,\ \tau\to 0,\ \|A^{-1}\|_2\to \pi^2\). Then,

      \[ \begin{aligned} \|e\|_2 &\le \|A^{-1}\|_2\|\tau\|_2 =\pi^2\|\tau\|_2 \\ \|\tau\|_2^2 &=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\tau_i^2 =\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}c^2\big(f''(x_j)\big)^2h^4. \end{aligned} \]

      If \(f''\) is bounded, \(\|f''\|\le \hat c\). Then, combing the constants and the sum, we have

      \[ \|\tau\|_2^2\le \hat c(N-1)h^4. \]

      Recall that \(N=\frac{1}{h}\). Then, \(\|\tau\|_2^2\le \hat c h^3 \Longrightarrow \|\tau\|_2\sim O(h^{3/2}).\) So,

      \[ \|e\|_2\sim O(h^{3/2}). \]

      • Practical Notes:

        When constructing \(A\), we first assemble the full matrix, and then replace the boundaries with actual BCs.

      • Another perspective of FD Convergence.

      Definition

      Definition 2.2.1 (\(h\)-Norm). Let \(v_h\) be a grid function.

      \[ \|v_h\|_h^2= \left(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1} v_j^2+\frac{1}{2}v_0^2+\frac{1}{2}v_N^2\right)h, \]

      and

      \[ L_h^0v_h=\frac{v_{j+1}-2v_j+v_{j-1}}{h^2}. \]

      Some properties include:

      1. \[ (L_h^0v_h,w_h)=(v_h,L_hw_h) \]

      2. \[ (L_h^0v_h,w_h)=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{v_{j+1}-v_j}{h}\cdot\frac{w_{j+1}-w_j}{h}. \]

      3. \(h(L_h^0v_h,v_h)\) is a norm. Denote this norm as \(|||v_h|||_h\).

      Proof 4.

      \[ h(L_h^0v_h,v_h)=h\sum\left(\frac{v_{j+1}-v_j}{h}\right)^2. \]

      Non-negativity and other properties can be easily shown.
      Q.E.D. \(\blacksquare\)

      1. \(c\|v_h\|_h^2\le |||v_h|||_h^2\).

      2. Problem: \(L_hu_h=f_h\).

      \[ \begin{aligned} c\|u_h\|_h^2 &\le h(L_hu_h,u_h)\le \|f_h\|_h\|u_h\|_h \Longrightarrow \|u_h\|\le c\|f_h\|_h \\ c\|e_h\|_h^2 &\le h(L_he_h,e_h)\le \|\tau\|_h\|e_h\|_h \Longrightarrow \|e_h\|_h\le \frac{1}{c}\|\tau\|_h. \end{aligned} \]

      \[ \begin{aligned} \|\tau\|_h^2 &=h\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N} c_j h^4\right| \le h\cdot N\cdot \max_{j=1,\ldots,N}c_j\cdot h^4 \\ &=h\cdot \frac{1}{h}\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}c_j\cdot h^4 =h^4\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}c_j, \end{aligned} \]

      so

      \[ \|\tau\|_h\le \sqrt{h^4\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}c_j} =h^2\sqrt{\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}c_j}\sim O(h^2). \]

      Remark

      Remark 5. (What’s Missing Here?).

      Differential Problem \(\xrightarrow{①}\) \(Au=b\) \(\xrightarrow{②}\) \(u_{\mathrm{num}}\approx A^{-1}b.\)
