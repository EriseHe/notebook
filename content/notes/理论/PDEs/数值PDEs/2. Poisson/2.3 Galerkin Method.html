      2.3 Galerkin Method

      2.3 Galerkin Methods

      Set-Up

      \[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=f \\ u(\partial\Omega)&=0 \end{aligned} \right. \]

      Weak formulation: Find \(u\) s.t.

      \[ \int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\,dx=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx \qquad \forall\, v\ \text{s.t.}\ v(\partial\Omega)=0. \]

      Requirements \(\nabla u\in L^2,\ \nabla v\in L^2\Longrightarrow u,v\in H_0^1,\) and \(f\in H^{-1}\) (dual of \(H_0^1\), functional applied to \(H_0^1\).)

      Lemma

      Lemma 2.1 Max-Milgram Lemma (Sufficient Condition) Consider the problem \(a(u,v)=F(v)\), where \(u,v\in X\). If

      • \(a(\cdot,\cdot)\) is a bilinear form,

      • bounded: \(\exists\, M>0\) s.t. \(|a(u,v)|\le M\|u\|_X\|v\|_X\),

      • coercivity: \(a(u,u)\ge \alpha\|u\|_X^2\ \forall\, u\in X\), and

      • \(F(\cdot)\) is a continuous linear functional: \(\|F(v)\|_X\le \gamma\|v\|_X\ \forall\, v\in X\),

      then, the problem is well-posed. i.e., ①②③④ \(\Longrightarrow\) well-posedness (sufficient but not necessary).

      Theorem

      Theorem 2.3.2

      Poisson equation is well-posed.

      Proof 1.

      \[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=f \\ u(\partial\Omega)&=0 \end{aligned} \right. \Longrightarrow \forall\, v\in H_0^1,\ \text{find }u\in H_0^1\ \text{s.t.}\ \underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v}_{a(u,v)} - \underbrace{\int_{\Omega}fv}_{F(v)} =0. \]

      • \(\|u\|_{H^1}^2=\|u\|_{L^2}^2+\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2\)

      • \[ \left|\int_{\Omega}fv\right|\le \|f\|_{L^2}\|v\|_{L^2}\le \|f\|_{H^1}\|v\|_{H^1} \]

      • \(\forall\, u\in X=H_0^1\),

        \[ \int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^2=a(u,u)\ge \alpha\|u\|_X^2. \]

      By Poincaré Inequality,

      \[ \begin{aligned} C_p\int_{\Omega}u^2&\le \int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^2 \\ C_p\|u\|_{L^2}^2&\le \|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2=\underbrace{|u|_{H^1}^2}_{semi\text{-}norm} \\ \|u\|_{H^1}^2 &=\int_{\Omega}u^2+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u^2 \le \left(\frac{1}{C_p}+1\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^2. \end{aligned} \]

      So, we have coercivity.

      By Lax-Milgram Lemma, Poisson equation is WP.
      Q.E.D. \(\blacksquare\)

      Remark

      Remark. \(a(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v\) is a scalar product in \(H_0^1\):

      • symmetric, and

      • non-negativity (by coercivity).

      The norm induced by \(a(u,u)=\|u\|_a^2\) is equivalent to \(\|u\|_{H^1}^2\):

      \[ C\|u\|_{H^1}^2\le a(u,u)\le \|u\|_{H^1}^2. \]

      If we change the problem and the space:

      \[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=f \\ \nabla u\cdot n&=0 \qquad \text{on }\partial\Omega \quad (\text{Neumann}) \end{aligned} \right. \]

      \[ \Longrightarrow\ \ \text{Find }u\in H^1\ \text{s.t.}\ \ \forall\, v\in H^1: \qquad \int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v=\int_{\Omega}fv. \]

      Now, we don’t have Poincaré Inequality anymore, and \(a(u,v)\) is not coercive. We cannot apply LM Lemma. In fact, the problem is not WP. Any \((u+\text{constant})\) will be a solution.

      Hw to make the problem WP?

      \[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\sigma u&=f,\qquad \sigma>0 \\ \nabla u\cdot n&=0 \qquad \text{on }\partial\Omega \end{aligned} \right. \]

      \[ \Longrightarrow\ \ \int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v+\sigma\int_{\Omega}uv=\int_{\Omega}fv \qquad \underbrace{}_{a(u,v)} \]

      \[ |a(u,v)|\le \max(\sigma,1)\|u\|_{H^1}\|v\|_{H^1}, \qquad \min(1,\sigma)\|u\|_{H^1}^2\le a(u,v). \]

      \(\Longrightarrow\) \(a(u,v)\) is coercive, and the problem is WP.

      Now, let’s consider mixed boundary conditions (i.e., Robin conditions).

      [figure omitted]

      \[ \left\{ \begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=f \\ u(\Gamma_D)&=0 \\ \nabla u\cdot n|_{\Gamma_N}&=0, \end{aligned} \right. \qquad \text{where }\Gamma_D\cup\Gamma_N=\partial\Omega,\ \text{and measure}(\Gamma_D\cap\Gamma_N)=0. \]

      \[ -\int_{\Omega}\Delta u\cdot v = -\underbrace{\int_{\Gamma_N}\nabla u\cdot n\,v}_{=0} +\underbrace{\int_{\Gamma_D}\nabla u\cdot n\,v}_{=0} +\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v. \]

      Problem: Find \(u\in H_{\Gamma_D}^1\) s.t. \(\forall\, v\in H_{\Gamma_D}^1\) s.t.

      \[ \int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v=\int_{\Omega}fv. \]

      This problem is WP because we have Poicaré on \(H_{\Gamma_D}^1\).

      Now, consider the true problem: Find \(u\in X=H_0^1\) s.t.

      \[ \int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla v=\int_{\Omega}fv \qquad \forall\, v\in X \Longrightarrow a(u,v)=F(v). \]

      Assumption \(a(u,v)=a(v,u)\) (symmetry) and all LM assumptions.

      The Galerkin Methods solve the numerical problem in \(X_N\subset X\): Find \(u_N\in X_N\) s.t.

      \[ a(u_N,v_N)=F(v_N), \qquad \forall\, v_N\in X_N. \]

      This problem is WP, which can be trivially proven by LM assumptions.

      Proposition

      Proposition 2.3 This problem is strongly consistent (consistency error is 0).

      Proof 2. Note that \(a(u,v)=F(v)\) and \(a(u_N,v_N)=F(v_N)\). Then,

      \[ a(u,v_N)=F(v_N) \qquad \text{because }X_N\subset X. \]

      The consistence error is 0. So, the problem is strongly consistent. We could also show

      \[ a(u-u_N,v_N)=0 \qquad \forall\, v_N\in X_N. \]

      Q.E.D. \(\blacksquare\)

      Proposition

      Proposition 2.4 This problem is stable.

      Proof 3.

      \[ \alpha\|u_N\|_{H^1}^2\le a(u_N,u_N)\le \|f\|\cdot \|u_N\|_{H^1}. \]

      So, it is clearly stable.
      Q.E.D. \(\blacksquare\)

      Lemma

      Lemma 2.5 Cèa Lemma

      \[ \|u-u_N\|_{H^1}\le K\inf_{w_N\in X_N}\|u-w_N\|_{H^1}. \]

      Proof 4.

      \[ \begin{aligned} \alpha\|u-u_N\|_{H^1}^2 &\le a(u-u_N,u-u_N) \\ &=a(u-u_N,u-w_N)+a(u-u_N,w_N-u_N) \qquad[\text{Bilinearity, } w_N\in X_N] \\ \alpha\|u-u_N\|_{H^1}^2 &\le a(u-u_N,u-w_N) \le M\|u-u_N\|_{H^1}\cdot \|u-w_N\|_{H^1} \qquad[\text{Continuity}] \\ \|u-u_N\|_{H^1} &\le \frac{M}{\alpha}\|u-w_N\|_{H^1} \\ \|u-u_N\|_{H^1} &\le K\|u-w_N\|_{H^1} \qquad \forall\, w_N\in X_N \\ \|u-u_N\|_{H^1} &\le K\inf_{w_N\in X_N}\|u-w_N\|_{H^1}. \end{aligned} \]

      Q.E.D. \(\blacksquare\)

      This Lemma implies that Galerkin solution is not the best one, but its \(H^1\) norm error goes to 0 as quick as the best one. i.e., it has the same rate of convergence as the best approximation in \(X_N\). When

      \[ N\to\infty,\ X_n\to X,\ \text{and}\ \inf_{w_N\in X_n}\|u-w_N\|_{H^1}\to 0. \]

      Claim

      Claim 2.6 \(P_N:\) Find \(u_N\in X_N,\ a(u_N,v_n)=F(v_N)\ \ \forall\, v_N\in X_N\) is WP.

      Proof 5. We can write

      \[ u_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_j\varphi_j(x), \]

      where \(\varphi_j(\cdot)\) are basis functions. Then,

      \[ X_n=\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_j\}_{j=0}^{N-1}. \]

      So,

      \[ \begin{aligned} a(u_N,v_N) &=a\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_j\varphi_j(x),v_N\right)=F(v_N) \\ \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_ja(\varphi_j,v_N) &=F(v_N) \qquad[\text{Bilinearity}] \end{aligned} \]

      Instead of testing on \(v_N\in X_N\), as \(X_N=\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_j\}_{j=0}^{N-1}\), we just need to prove it works for all \(\varphi_i\)’s.

      \[ \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_ja(\varphi_j,\varphi_i)=F(\varphi_i) \qquad[\text{Take }v_N=\varphi_i] \]

      Define

      \[ A:=\big[a(\varphi_j,\varphi_i)\big]_{i,j}, \qquad c=\big[c_j\big]_j, \qquad b=\big[F(\varphi_i)\big]_i. \]

      So, \(P_N\) becomes

      \[ Ac=b. \]

      Claim

      Claim 2.7 \(A\) is non-singular

      • \(A\) is symmetric: \(a(\varphi_j,\varphi_i)=a(\varphi_i,\varphi_j)\qquad[\text{symmetry}]\)

      • \(A\) is positive-definite: Assume \(c\ne 0\), then

      \[ \begin{aligned} c^\top Ac &=\sum_{i,j} c_jA_{ij}c_i =\sum_{i,j} c_j a(\varphi_j,\varphi_i)c_i \\ &=\sum_{i,j} a(c_j\varphi_j,c_i\varphi_i) \qquad[\text{bilinearity}] \\ &=a\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}c_j\varphi_j,\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}c_i\varphi_i\right) \qquad[\text{bilinearity}] \\ &=a(u_N,u_N) \qquad\left[u_N=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}c_i\varphi_i\right] \\ &\ge \alpha\|u_N\|_{H^1}^2\ge 0 \qquad[\text{coercivity}] \end{aligned} \]

      So, \(A\) is SPD, and thus \(A\) is non-singular. \(\Longrightarrow\ P_N\) is WP.
      Q.E.D. \(\blacksquare\)
