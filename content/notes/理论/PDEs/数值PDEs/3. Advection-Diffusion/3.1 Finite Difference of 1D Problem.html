      3.1 Finite Difference of 1D Problem

      3.1.1 Analytic Solution

      Consider 1D Diffusion-convection problem with dirichlet boundary

      \[ \left\{\begin{array}{l} -\mu u^{\prime \prime}+\beta u^{\prime}=0, \quad \mu \geq 0 \\ u(0)=0 \quad \text { and } \quad u(1)=1 \end{array}\right. \]

      Then, the analytic solution is \[ \boxed{u = \frac{e^{\beta/\mu x}-1}{e^{\beta/\mu}-1}} \]

      3.1.2 Finite Difference Discretization

      For complex real-world problems, we discretize the continuous equation into a grid of points for computer to solve it algebraically.

      By Taylor expansion on the neighboring points of \(u_{i}\), we obtain \[ \begin{aligned} & u_{i+1}=u_i+h u_i^{\prime}+\frac{h^2}{2} u_i^{\prime \prime}+\frac{h^3}{6} u_i^{\prime \prime \prime}+\mathcal{O}\left(h^4\right) \\ & u_{i-1}=u_i-h u_i^{\prime}+\frac{h^2}{2} u_i^{\prime \prime}-\frac{h^3}{6} u_i^{\prime \prime \prime}+\mathcal{O}\left(h^4\right) \end{aligned} \] Then, we first derive the second-derivative diffusion term: \[ \begin{align} u_{i+1} + u_{i-1} & = 2u_{i} + h^{2}u''_{i} & & + \mathcal{O}(h^4) \\ h^{2}u''_{i} & =u_{i+1} -2u_{i} + u_{i-1} & & + \mathcal{O(h^4)}\\ u''_{i} & =\frac{u_{i+1}-2u_{i} + u_{i+1} }{h^{2}} & & +\mathcal{O}(h^{2}) \end{align} \] we next derive the first-derivative convection term: \[ \begin{align} u_{i+1}-u_{i-1} & =2 h u_i^{\prime}+\frac{h^3}{3} u_i^{\prime \prime \prime} & & +\mathcal{O}\left(h^4\right) \\ 2 h u_i^{\prime} & =u_{i+1}-u_{i-1}-\frac{h^3}{3} u_i^{\prime \prime \prime} & & +\mathcal{O}\left(h^4\right) \\ u_i^{\prime} & =\frac{u_{i+1}-u_{i-1}}{2 h}-\frac{h^2}{6} u_i^{\prime \prime \prime} & & +\mathcal{O}\left(h^3\right) \\ u_i^{\prime} & =\frac{u_{i+1}-u_{i-1}}{2 h} & & +\mathcal{O}\left(h^2\right) \end{align} \] Finally, we obtain the discretization: \[ \begin{aligned} -\mu\underbrace{ \left(\frac{u_{i+1}-2 u_i+u_{i-1}}{h^{2}} \right) }_{ u^{\prime \prime} \sim \mathcal{O}(h^{2})}+ \beta\underbrace{ \left(\frac{u_{i+1}-u_{i-1}}{2 h} \right) }_{ u' \sim\mathcal{O}(h^{2})}& = 0 \end{aligned} \]

      3.1.2 Stability and physical reliability

      To solve this for all grid points, we group the coefficients associated with each specific point (\(u_{i+1}, u_i, u_{i-1}\)) from the last formula:

      \[ \begin{align} \left(-\frac{\mu}{h^2} u_{i+1}+\frac{2 \mu}{h^2} u_i-\frac{\mu}{h^2} u_{i-1}\right)+\left(\frac{\beta}{2 h} u_{i+1}-\frac{\beta}{2 h} u_{i-1}\right) & =0\\ \underbrace{\left(\frac{\mu}{h^2}-\frac{\beta}{2 h}\right)}_a u_{i+1}-\underbrace{\frac{2 \mu}{h^2}}_b u_i+\underbrace{\left(\frac{\mu}{h^2}+\frac{\beta}{2 h}\right)}_c u_{i-1} & =0 \end{align} \]

      with boundary conditions \(u_0=0\) and \({} u_N=1\).

      this turns into a second-order difference equation, which allows us to find the “roots” of the characteristic equation to see if solution stays smooth or oscillate.

      We then have a characteristic equation, \[ \begin{align} & a \rho^2+b \rho+c=0\\ & a=\frac{\mu}{h^2}-\frac{\beta}{2 h}, \quad b=-\frac{2 \mu}{h^2}, \quad c=\frac{\mu}{h^2}+\frac{\beta}{2 h} \end{align} \] where we obtain the solution as \[ \rho_1=1, \quad \rho_2=\frac{c}{a}=\frac{\mu / h^2+\beta /(2 h)}{\mu / h^2-\beta /(2 h)}=\frac{1+\frac{\beta h}{2 \mu}}{1-\frac{\beta h}{2 \mu}} \] The behavior of this numerical solution depends the sign of \(\rho\). To ensure it is physically realistic (smooth), we observe \[ \begin{aligned} & \frac{\beta h}{2 \mu}<1 \Longrightarrow \rho_2>0 \quad \Longrightarrow\;\; \text { no sign-flip oscillation } \\ & \frac{\beta h}{2 \mu}>1 \Longrightarrow \rho_2<0 \quad \Longrightarrow \;\;\text { zig-zag oscillation. } \end{aligned} \] From here, we obtain the Pèclet Number \(\left(\mathbb{P}_e\right)\) by this condition:

      DefinitionPéclet number

      For the 1D convection-diffusion problem, \(-\mu u''+\beta u'=0\), the Cell Péclet number is defined by \[ \mathbb{P}_e=\frac{|\beta|\,h}{2\mu} \]

      Remark

      Péclet number measures the relative strength of convection to diffusion on one mesh cell: \[ \begin{align} \mathbb{P}_e & \ll 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{diffusion-dominated}\\ \mathbb{P}_e & \gg 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{convection-dominated} \end{align} \]

      For the centered finite-difference scheme, the standard non-oscillation condition is \[ \mathbb{P}_e\le 1. \]

      Problem: If \(\frac{\mu}{\beta}=10^{-6}\), since we require step size: \[ \frac{\beta h}{2 \mu}<1 \Longrightarrow h<\frac{2 \mu}{\beta} \] then we would need over 1 million points…

      3.1.3 Upwind Scheme

      The centered difference scheme forces us to use a tiny grid size. Can we have a better scheme?

      Yes, the famous workaround is upwind scheme that breaks the symmetry.

      Remark

      Information travels with the wind.

      If the wind is blowing from left to right (\(\beta > 0\)), what happens at point \(i\) is mostly influenced by what is coming from behind it (point \(i-1\)). So, we approximate the advection term using only a backward difference:\[\boxed{\beta u' \approx \beta \frac{u_i - u_{i-1}}{h}}\]

      1. accuracy drops from \(\mathcal{O}(h^2)\) to \(\mathcal{O}(h)\)
      2. mathematically sacrifice, but *physically consistent

      3.1.3.1 Stability

      Theorem

      upwind scheme guarantees that the solution never oscillate, regardless of grid size \(h\) or wind speed \(\beta\).

      3.1.3.2 Proof.

      PART 1. We start with the definition of the upwind derivative \[ \begin{align} \beta \frac{{u_{i}-u_{i-1}}}{h } & = \frac{\beta}{2h}(2u_{i}-2u_{i-1}) \\ & = \frac{\beta}{2h}(u_{i+1}-u_{i-1}-u_{i-1}+2u_{i}-u_{i-1}) \\ & = \beta \underbrace{ \frac{{u_{i+1}-u_{i-1}}}{2h} }_{ u'_{\text{center}} }- \frac{\beta h}{2} \underbrace{ \left( \frac{{u_{i-1} -2u_{i}+u_{i-1}}}{h^2} \right) }_{ u'' } \\ \end{align} \] That is, \[ \beta u'_{\text{\scriptsize UPW}}= \beta u'_{\text{center}} - \frac{\beta h}{2}u'' \] PART 2. Next, we plug back to the original equation to get a perturbed upwind problem:

      \[ \begin{align} -\mu u'' & + \beta u'_{\text{\scriptsize UPW}} & = 0 \\ -\mu u'' & + \beta \left( u'_{\text{center}} - \frac{ h}{2}u'' \right) & =0 \\ -\left( \mu +\frac{\beta h}{2} \right)u'' & + \beta u'_{\text{center}} & =0 \end{align} \] Here, recall that \[ \mathbb{P}_{e} = \frac{\beta h}{2\mu} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad\mu\mathbb{P}_{e} = \frac{\beta h}{2} \] So substitute into, we have

      \[ \begin{align} -\left( \mu +\mu \mathbb{P}_{e} \right)u'' + \beta u'_{\text{center}} & =0 \\ -\underbrace{ \mu\left( 1 + \mathbb{P}_{e} \right) }_{ \mu' }u'' + \beta u'_{\text{center}} & =0 \end{align} \] Hence, the Peclet number of the problems under upwind scheme becomes: \[ \mathbb{P}^{\text{\scriptsize UPW}}_{e}=\mathbb{P}^{*}_{e} = \frac{\beta h}{2\mu^{*}}= \frac{\beta h}{2\mu(1+\mathbb{P}_{e})} = \frac{\mathbb{P}_{e}}{1+\mathbb{P}_{e}}<1 \] since \(\mathbb{P}_{e } > 0\) is always true.

      ■.

      3.1.3.2 Consistency

      We discovered that using the upwind scheme is mathematically identical to using the centered scheme on a “perturbed” physical problem.

      Is this method consistent?

      Yes, notice that the slight perturbation depends on grid size \(h\), so \[ \lim _{h \rightarrow 0}\left(\mu+\frac{\beta h}{2}\right)=\mu+0=\mu \] which returns to the original problem. Since upwind is both stable and consistent, Lax’s Equivalence Theorem guarantees that the numerical solution would converge to the true physical solution!

      Remark

      \[ \text { upwind }=\operatorname{centered}\left(\mu\left(1+\mathbb{P}_e\right)\right) \]

      3.1.4 Second-order Upwind

      We build a “smarter” upwind scheme. Usin Taylor’s expansion to design a second order approximation of \(u_i^{\prime}\), using only \(u_{i-2}\) and \(u_{i-1}\).

      The standard upwind scheme only looks one step behind (\(u_{i-1}\)), which gives you poor, first-order accuracy \(\mathcal{O}(h)\). We can design a scheme that reaches two steps behind (\(u_{i-1}\) and \(u_{i-2}\)) to achieve second-order accuracy \(\mathcal{O}(h^2)\), while still preventing oscillations.

      3.1.4.1 Write the Taylor Expansions

      We want to approximate the first derivative \(u'_i\) at the point \(x_i\). We start by writing the Taylor series for the two upwind points, \(u_{i-1}\) and \(u_{i-2}\), similarly,

      \[ \begin{aligned} u_{i-1} &= u_i - h u'_i + \frac{h^2}{2} u''_i - \frac{h^3}{6} u'''_i &&+ \mathcal{O}(h^4) \\ u_{i-2} &= u_i - (2h) u'_i + \frac{(2h)^2}{2} u''_i - \frac{(2h)^3}{6} u'''_i &&+ \mathcal{O}(h^4)\\ &= u_i - 2h u'_i + 2h^2 u''_i - \frac{8h^3}{6} u'''_i &&+ \mathcal{O}(h^4) \end{aligned} \]

      3.1.4.2 Cancel the Second Derivative

      Now, we subtract the \(u_{i-2}\) equation from our newly multiplied \(4u_{i-1}\) equation: \[ \begin{aligned} 4u_{i-1} &= 4u_i - 4h u'_i + 2h^2 u''_i - \frac{4h^3}{6} u'''_i \\ -\quad u_{i-2} &= u_i - 2h u'_i + 2h^2 u''_i - \frac{8h^3}{6} u'''_i \\ \hline 4u_{i-1} - u_{i-2} &= (4u_i - u_i) + (-4h u'_i - (-2h u'_i)) + (2h^2 u''_i - 2h^2 u''_i) + \text{Remainder} \end{aligned} \]

      Notice how the \(u''_i\) terms completely vanish! The math simplifies beautifully to: \[4u_{i-1} - u_{i-2} = 3u_i - 2h u'_i + \mathcal{O}(h^3)\]

      3.1.4.3 Solve for \(u'_i\)

      Now that the second derivative is gone, we just use basic algebra to isolate the first derivative \(u'_i\). \[ \begin{align} 2h u'_i & = 3u_i - 4u_{i-1} + u_{i-2} & & + \mathcal{O}(h^3) \\ u'_i & = \frac{3u_i - 4u_{i-1} + u_{i-2}}{2h} & & + \frac{\mathcal{O}(h^3)}{2h} \end{align} \] Here, we obtain the final Second-Order Upwind Formula: \[\boxed{ u'_i = \frac{3u_i - 4u_{i-1} + u_{i-2}}{2h} + \mathcal{O}(h^2) }\]

      3.1.3.4 Why is this so powerful?

      By adding just one extra node behind your current point (\(u_{i-2}\)), you have successfully upgraded the accuracy from \(\mathcal{O}(h)\) to \(\mathcal{O}(h^2)\).

      Because you are still exclusively gathering information from the “upwind” side (the side the wind is blowing from), you retain the stability of the upwind scheme and avoid those zig-zag oscillations, but your solution will no longer suffer from severe artificial smearing.

      3.1.5 Backward Upwind

      If \(\beta<0\), the wind is backward. Use

      \[ \frac{u_{i+1}-u_i}{h} . \]
