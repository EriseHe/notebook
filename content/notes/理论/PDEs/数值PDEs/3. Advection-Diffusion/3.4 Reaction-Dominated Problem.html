      3.4 Reaction-Dominated Problem

      3.4.1 Reaction-Dominated Problem

      Consider the boundary value problem \[ \begin{aligned} -\mu u'' + \sigma u &= 0, \qquad x\in (0,1),\\ u(0)&=0,\\ u(1)&=1. \end{aligned} \] Let \[ 0=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_N=1, \qquad h=x_i-x_{i-1}=\frac1N, \] and let \(\{\psi_i\}\) be the standard piecewise linear hat basis functions. For an interior node \(x_i\), \(i=1,\dots,N-1\), we have \[ \psi_i(x)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{x-x_{i-1}}{h}, & x\in [x_{i-1},x_i],\\[1ex] \dfrac{x_{i+1}-x}{h}, & x\in [x_i,x_{i+1}],\\[1ex] 0, & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

      3.4.1.1 Weak Form

      The weak form is: find \(u_h\in V_h\) such that for all \(v_h\in V_h^0\), \[ \mu \int_0^1 u_h' v_h'\,dx +\sigma \int_0^1 u_h v_h\,dx =0. \] Taking \(v_h=\psi_i\) gives, for each interior node \(i\), \[ \mu \int_0^1 u_h' \psi_i'\,dx +\sigma \int_0^1 u_h \psi_i\,dx =0. \]

      3.4.2 Standard \(P_1\) Finite Element Discretization

      Using the usual stiffness and mass matrices \[ \underbrace{ \frac{\mu}{h}\left[\begin{array}{cccc} 2 & -1 & & \\ -1 & 2 & -1 & \\ & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots \\ & & -1 & 2 \end{array}\right]}_{\text{stiffness}} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \underbrace{ \frac{\sigma h}{6}\left[\begin{array}{cccc} 4 & 1 & & \\ 1 & 4 & 1 & \\ & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots \\ & & 1 & 4 \end{array}\right]}_{\text{consistent mass}} \] the nodal equation at an interior node is \[ \begin{align} \frac{\mu}{h}\,(-u_{i-1}+2u_i-u_{i+1}) +\frac{\sigma h}{6}(u_{i-1}+4u_i+u_{i+1}) & =0\\ -\mu\left(\frac{u_{i+1}-2u_i+u_{i-1}}{h^2}\right) +\frac{\sigma}{6}\left(u_{i-1}+4u_i+u_{i+1}\right) & =0 \\ \end{align} \] and equivalently, \[ \left(-\frac{\mu}{h^2}+\frac{\sigma}{6}\right)u_{i-1} +\left(\frac{2\mu}{h^2}+\frac{4\sigma}{6}\right)u_i +\left(-\frac{\mu}{h^2}+\frac{\sigma}{6}\right)u_{i+1} =0 \] To keep the off-diagonal entries nonpositive, we require \[ \begin{align} -\frac{\mu}{h^2}+\frac{\sigma}{6}&\le 0\\ \mathbb{P}_{e}=\frac{\sigma h^2}{6\mu}&\le 1&& \end{align} \Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{h\le \sqrt{\frac{6\mu}{\sigma}}} \]

      for mesh restriction. ## Reaction-Dominated Problem

      Consider \[ \begin{aligned} -\mu u'' + \sigma u &= 0, \qquad x\in (0,1),\\ u(0)&=0,\\ u(1)&=1. \end{aligned} \]

      Let \[ 0=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_N=1, \qquad h=x_i-x_{i-1}=\frac{1}{N}, \] and let \(\{\varphi_i\}\) be the standard piecewise linear hat basis functions.

      3.4.3 Weak Form

      The weak form is: find \(u_h\) such that \[ \mu \int_0^1 u_h' v_h'\,dx+\sigma \int_0^1 u_h v_h\,dx=0 \] for all test functions \(v_h\).

      Taking \(v_h=\varphi_i\), we obtain \[ \mu \int_0^1 u_h' \varphi_i'\,dx+\sigma \int_0^1 u_h \varphi_i\,dx=0. \]

      3.4.4 Standard Finite Element Equation

      Using the usual stiffness and consistent mass matrices, the nodal equation at an interior node \(x_i\) is \[ \frac{\mu}{h}(-u_{i-1}+2u_i-u_{i+1}) +\frac{\sigma h}{6}(u_{i-1}+4u_i+u_{i+1})=0. \]

      Dividing by \(h\), this becomes \[ -\mu\left(\frac{u_{i+1}-2u_i+u_{i-1}}{h^2}\right) +\frac{\sigma}{6}(u_{i-1}+4u_i+u_{i+1})=0. \]

      Equivalently, \[ \left(-\frac{\mu}{h^2}+\frac{\sigma}{6}\right)u_{i-1} +\left(\frac{2\mu}{h^2}+\frac{4\sigma}{6}\right)u_i +\left(-\frac{\mu}{h^2}+\frac{\sigma}{6}\right)u_{i+1}=0. \]

      To keep the off-diagonal coefficients nonpositive, we require \[ -\frac{\mu}{h^2}+\frac{\sigma}{6}\le 0, \] that is, \[ \frac{\sigma h^2}{6\mu}\le 1. \]

      Hence, \[ h\le \sqrt{\frac{6\mu}{\sigma}}. \]

      3.4.5 Mass Lumping

      We now approximate the mass term by the trapezoidal rule, written in your notation as \(\sigma(T)\).

      3.4.5.1 Off-diagonal term

      For \(j\neq i\), only the neighboring cases \(j=i-1\) and \(j=i+1\) need to be checked, since otherwise the supports do not overlap and the integral is automatically \(0\). For example, \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma \int_0^1 \varphi_i\varphi_j &\approx \sigma(T)\int_0^1 \varphi_i\varphi_j \\ &= \sigma(T)\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i+1}} \varphi_i\varphi_j \\ &= \sigma(T)\left( \int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_i}\varphi_i\varphi_{i-1} +\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}}\varphi_i\varphi_{i-1} +\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_i}\varphi_i\varphi_{i+1} +\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}}\varphi_i\varphi_{i+1} \right) \\ &= \sigma(T)\left( \int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_i}\varphi_i\varphi_{i-1} +0 +0 +\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}}\varphi_i\varphi_{i+1} \right), \end{aligned} \] since \[ \int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}}\varphi_i\varphi_{i-1}=0, \qquad \int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_i}\varphi_i\varphi_{i+1}=0, \] because the supports do not overlap on those intervals.

      Applying the trapezoidal rule on each remaining interval, \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma \int_0^1 \varphi_i\varphi_j &\approx \sigma(T)\left( \int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_i}\varphi_i\varphi_{i-1} + \int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}}\varphi_i\varphi_{i+1} \right) \\ &= \frac{\sigma h}{2}\Big(\varphi_i\varphi_{i-1}(x_{i-1})+\varphi_i\varphi_{i-1}(x_i)\Big) + \frac{\sigma h}{2}\Big(\varphi_i\varphi_{i+1}(x_i)+\varphi_i\varphi_{i+1}(x_{i+1})\Big) \\ &= 0+0 \\ &= 0. \end{aligned} \]

      Therefore, the off-diagonal terms vanish: \[ \sigma(T)\int_0^1 \varphi_i\varphi_j = 0, \qquad j\neq i. \] ### Diagonal term

      For the diagonal term, \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma \int_0^1 \varphi_i^2 &\approx \sigma(T)\int_0^1 \varphi_i^2 \\ &= \sigma(T)\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_i}\varphi_i^2 +\sigma(T)\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}}\varphi_i^2. \end{aligned} \]

      Applying the trapezoidal rule, \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma(T)\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_i}\varphi_i^2 &= \frac{\sigma h}{2}\Big(\varphi_i^2(x_{i-1})+\varphi_i^2(x_i)\Big), \\ \sigma(T)\int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}}\varphi_i^2 &= \frac{\sigma h}{2}\Big(\varphi_i^2(x_i)+\varphi_i^2(x_{i+1})\Big). \end{aligned} \]

      Since \[ \varphi_i(x_{i-1})=0,\qquad \varphi_i(x_i)=1,\qquad \varphi_i(x_{i+1})=0, \] we get \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma(T)\int_0^1 \varphi_i^2 &= \frac{\sigma h}{2}(0+1)+\frac{\sigma h}{2}(1+0) \\ &= \sigma h. \end{aligned} \]

      So the lumped mass matrix is diagonal, with diagonal entry \(\sigma h\).

      3.4.6 Lumped Scheme

      Therefore, after mass lumping, the finite element equation becomes \[ \frac{\mu}{h}(-u_{i-1}+2u_i-u_{i+1})+\sigma h\,u_i=0. \]

      Dividing by \(h\) gives \[ -\mu\left(\frac{u_{i+1}-2u_i+u_{i-1}}{h^2}\right)+\sigma u_i=0. \]

      Thus the lumped finite element scheme agrees with the finite difference scheme.

      3.4.7 Bilinear Form

      For this reaction–diffusion problem, the bilinear form is \[ a(\varphi_j,\varphi_i) = \mu\int_0^1 \varphi_j'\varphi_i'\,dx +\sigma\int_0^1 \varphi_j\varphi_i\,dx. \]

      3.4.3 Mass Lumping

      To lump the mass matrix, we approximate the mass integral by nodal quadrature (equivalently, the trapezoidal rule on each element). For the \(i\)-th test function, \[ \begin{align} \int_0^1 u \psi_i\,dx = & \int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_i} u\psi_i\,dx + \int_{x_i}^{x_{i+1}} u\psi_i\,dx \\ = & \frac{h}{2}\Big[\cancel{ u\underbrace{ \psi_i(x_{i-1}) }_{ 0 }}+u\underbrace{ \psi_i(x_i) }_{ 1 }\Big] + \frac{h}{2}\Big[u\underbrace{ \psi_i(x_i) }_{ 1 }+u\cancel{ \underbrace{ \psi_i(x_{i+1}) }_{ 0 } }\Big] & & \text{trapezoidal rule}\\ = & \frac{h}{2}u_{i}+ \frac{h}{2}u_i = hu_{i} \end{align} \] So the lumped mass contribution is \[ \boxed{\sigma \int_0^1 u\psi_i\,dx \approx \sigma h\,u_i} \]

      3.4.8 Lumped-Mass Finite Element Scheme

      Replacing the consistent mass term by the lumped one gives \[ \begin{align} \frac{\mu}{h}(-u_{i-1}+2u_i-u_{i+1})+\sigma h\,u_i & =0 \\ -\mu\left(\frac{u_{i+1}-2u_i+u_{i-1}}{h^2}\right)+\sigma u_i&=0 \end{align} \] This is exactly the standard finite difference discretization: \[ \boxed{ \text{FD}=\text{FE}+\text{mass lumping}. } \]

      3.4.8.1 Bilinear Form for Reaction-Diffusion

      For the reaction–diffusion problem, the bilinear form is \[ a(\psi_j,\psi_i) = \mu\int_0^1 \psi_j'\psi_i'\,dx +\sigma\int_0^1 \psi_j\psi_i\,dx. \]

      To record the finite element bilinear form: \[ \boxed{ a(\psi_j,\psi_i) = \mu\int_0^1 \psi_j'\psi_i'\,dx +\sigma\int_0^1 \psi_j\psi_i\,dx. } \] ### Lumped Bilinear Form

      The consistent finite element bilinear form for the reaction–diffusion problem is \[ a(w,v) = \mu\int_0^1 w'v'\,dx + \sigma\int_0^1 wv\,dx. \]

      After mass lumping, we replace the exact mass integral by a nodal quadrature rule: \[ \int_0^1 wv\,dx \;\approx\; Q_h(wv). \] Thus the lumped bilinear form becomes \[ \boxed{ a_h(w,v) = \mu\int_0^1 w'v'\,dx + \sigma Q_h(wv). } \]

      For piecewise linear basis functions \(\{\psi_i\}\), the nodal quadrature gives \[ Q_h(\psi_j\psi_i)=h\,\delta_{ij}, \] so \[ \boxed{ a_h(\psi_j,\psi_i) = \mu\int_0^1 \psi_j'\psi_i'\,dx + \sigma h\,\delta_{ij}. } \]

      Hence the mass-lumped finite element matrix is \[ A_h = K + \sigma M_{\mathrm{lump}}, \] where \(K\) is the usual stiffness matrix and \[ M_{\mathrm{lump}} = hI. \]

      So in 1D with \(P_1\) elements, \[ \boxed{ M_{\mathrm{lump}} = hI, \qquad K = \frac{\mu}{h} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 & & \\ -1 & 2 & -1 & \\ & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots \\ & & -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}. } \]

      3.4.8.2 Why Strang’s Lemma Appears

      The original finite element method uses the exact bilinear form \(a(\cdot,\cdot)\), but mass lumping replaces it by the approximate form \(a_h(\cdot,\cdot)\). So this is not exactly the same Galerkin method anymore: it is a perturbed variational problem.

      That is why Strang’s Lemma is the right tool. It says that the total error is controlled by

      1. the usual finite element approximation error, and
      2. the additional consistency error coming from replacing \(a\) by \(a_h\).

      In this setting, if \(u\) is the exact solution and \(u_h\) is the lumped-mass finite element solution, then schematically \[ \|u-u_h\| \;\le\; C\left( \underbrace{\inf_{v_h\in V_h}\|u-v_h\|}_{\text{approximation error}} + \underbrace{\sup_{w_h\in V_h\setminus\{0\}} \frac{|a(v_h,w_h)-a_h(v_h,w_h)|}{\|w_h\|}}_{\text{quadrature / lumping error}} \right). \]

      So the full picture is: \[ \text{mass lumping} = \text{exact mass bilinear form $\to$ quadrature-based diagonal approximation} \]

      And Strang’s Lemma tells us that this is acceptable as long as the added quadrature error is small enough.

      3.4.8.3 Summary

      • The consistent bilinear form is \[ a(w,v)=\mu\int_0^1 w'v'\,dx+\sigma\int_0^1 wv\,dx. \]
      • The lumped bilinear form is \[ a_h(w,v)=\mu\int_0^1 w'v'\,dx+\sigma Q_h(wv). \]
      • For \(P_1\) elements in 1D, \[ Q_h(\psi_j\psi_i)=h\delta_{ij}, \] so the mass matrix becomes diagonal.
      • Therefore, the standard finite difference scheme can be interpreted as \[ \boxed{\text{FD}=\text{FE}+\text{mass lumping}.} \]

      4. Strang’s First Lemma

      Let \(u\) be the exact solution and \(\tilde u_h\) the discrete solution defined by the approximate bilinear form \(a_h\).
      Then, under the usual continuity and stability assumptions, \[ \|u-\tilde u_h\|_V \le C \left( \underbrace{\inf_{v_h\in V_h}\|u-v_h\|_V}_{\text{best approximation}} + \underbrace{\sup_{w_h\in V_h\setminus\{0\}} \frac{|a(v_h,w_h)-a_h(v_h,w_h)|}{\|w_h\|_V}}_{\text{bilinear form consistency error}} + \underbrace{\sup_{w_h\in V_h\setminus\{0\}} \frac{|F(w_h)-F_h(w_h)|}{\|w_h\|_V}}_{\text{load consistency error}} \right). \]

      This is the full picture:

      \[ \text{total error} = \text{approximation error} + \text{quadrature / mass-lumping error}. \]

      5. Interpretation for mass lumping

      Mass lumping changes the discrete problem by replacing the exact mass/reaction inner product with a simpler quadrature rule, often producing a diagonal matrix.

      So Strang’s Lemma tells us:

      • if \(V_h\) approximates well, and
      • if the quadrature error in \(a_h\) and \(F_h\) is small,

      then the total error remains controlled.

      In other words, \[ \boxed{ \text{mass lumping is acceptable if the added consistency error is of higher order or comparable to the FE approximation error.} } \]

      6. Practical note for your matrix entries

      For the convection-diffusion-reaction problem, the basis-entry formula you want in your notes is \[ A_{ij} = \mu \int_0^1 \psi_j' \psi_i' \, dx + \beta \int_0^1 \psi_j' \psi_i \, dx + \sigma \int_0^1 \psi_j \psi_i \, dx. \]

      If you then apply mass lumping only to the mass/reaction term, replace \[ \int_0^1 \psi_j \psi_i\,dx \quad \text{by} \quad Q_h(\psi_j\psi_i), \] so that \[ A_{ij}^{\mathrm{lump}} = \mu \int_0^1 \psi_j' \psi_i' \, dx + \beta \int_0^1 \psi_j' \psi_i \, dx + \sigma\, Q_h(\psi_j\psi_i). \]

      This is exactly the setting where Strang’s Lemma is the right tool.
