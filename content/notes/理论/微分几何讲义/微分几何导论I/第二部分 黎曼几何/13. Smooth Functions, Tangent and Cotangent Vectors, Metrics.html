13. Smooth Functions, Tangent and Cotangent Vectors, Metrics

2025-10-08

1 Smooth functions on a manifold

Let \(M\) be a smooth manifold. A map \(f:M\to\mathbb R\) is smooth if for any coordinate map \[ \sigma:U\subset\mathbb R^{n}\to M \] the composition \[ f\circ\sigma:U\to\mathbb R \] is a \(C^\infty\) function. We write \[ C^\infty(M)=\{\text{smooth functions }M\to\mathbb R\} \] (Smoothness for maps between manifolds is defined the same way, in local coordinates.)

2 Tangent vectors

Definition.
A tangent vector \(X\in T_pM\) is a directional derivative \[ X:C^\infty(M)\to\mathbb R \] such that \[ X(fg)=f(p)\,Xg+g(p)\,Xf \] for all \(f,g\in C^\infty(M)\). As in the surface case, \(T_pM\) is an \(n\)–dimensional vector space. In local coordinates \((x^1,\dots,x^n)\), the coordinate basis for \(T_pM\) is given by \[ \partial_i:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}, \qquad i=1,\dots,n \] For a curve \(\gamma:I\to M\), the velocity is the tangent vector \[ \dot\gamma:f\longmapsto\frac{d}{dt}\big(f\circ\gamma\big) \] at the point \(\gamma(t)\).

2.1 Tangent bundle and vector bundles

The space \[ TM=\bigcup_{p\in M}T_pM \] has the structure of a manifold. Given a coordinate patch \[ \sigma:U\to M \] we define \[ \widetilde\sigma:U\times\mathbb R^n\longrightarrow TM \] by \[ \widetilde\sigma(x,v)=v^j\,\partial_j\big|_{\sigma(x)}\in T_{\sigma(x)}M \]

\(TM\) is called the tangent bundle of \(M\).

2.2 Vector bundles

Definition.
A vector bundle \(E\) of rank \(k\) over \(M\) is a manifold \(E\) equipped with a smooth map \[ \pi:E\to M \] such that

  1. The fiber \[ E_p:=\pi^{-1}(p) \] is a \(k\)–dimensional vector space for each \(p\in M\)

  2. For each \(p\in M\), there is a neighborhood \(V\ni p\) and a diffeomorphism \[ \psi:\pi^{-1}(V)\longrightarrow V\times\mathbb R^k \]

A section of \(E\) is a smooth map \[ f:M\to E \] such that \(f(p)\in E_p\) for each \(p\) (i.e. \(\pi\circ f=\operatorname{id}_M\)).

A section of \(TM\) is called a vector field.

3 Cotangent vectors and the cotangent bundle

For a finite–dimensional vector space \(V\), the dual space \(V^*\) is the set of linear maps \[ V\to\mathbb R \]

The cotangent space \[ T_p^*M=(T_pM)^* \] is the dual space of \(T_pM\). Given coordinates \(x^i\) near \(p\), we have the coordinate basis \(\partial_j\) for \(T_pM\). The dual basis \(dx^i\) is defined by \[ dx^i(\partial_j)=\delta^i{}_j \] This allows us to define coordinate patches for \[ T^*M=\bigcup_{p\in M}T_p^*M \] the cotangent bundle of \(M\).

4 Differential of a smooth function

For \(f\in C^\infty(M)\), the differential of \(f\) is the section \(df\in\Gamma(T^*M)\), defined by \[ df(X)=Xf \] for \(X\in T_pM\).

This is consistent with the cotangent basis notation \[ dx^i(\partial_k) :=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^k}(x^i) =\delta^i{}_k \]

5 Riemannian metrics

A metric on \(M\) is a smooth family of inner products \[ g(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,):T_pM\times T_pM\to\mathbb R \] In local coordinates, this means that \[ g_{ij}(x):=g(\partial_i,\partial_j) \] are smooth functions of \((x^1,\dots,x^n)\) and the matrix \((g_{ij})\) is symmetric and positive definite at each point. The metric defines the length of curves \(\gamma:I\to M\) by \[ \mathcal{l}(\gamma)=\int_I\lvert\dot\gamma\rvert\,dt \] where \(\lvert\dot\gamma\rvert^2=g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)\).

6 Metric as an isomorphism \(TM\leftrightarrow T^*M\)

The metric defines a map \[ T_pM\longrightarrow T_p^*M, \qquad X\longmapsto g(X,\cdot) \]

In coordinates, \[ \partial_i\longmapsto g(\partial_i,\cdot)=g_{ij}\,dx^j \]

Similarly, we can use the inverse matrix \[ [g^{ij}]=[g_{ij}]^{-1} \] to define a map \[ g^{-1}:T_p^*M\longrightarrow T_pM \]

7 Gradient vector field

Example.
The gradient vector field of \(f\) is defined by \[ \nabla f:=g^{-1}(df) \] which means \[ g(\nabla f,\cdot)=df(\cdot) \]

In coordinates, \[ \begin{aligned} df &= (\partial_j f)\,dx^j \\[4pt] \nabla f &= g^{ij}\,(\partial_j f)\,\partial_i \end{aligned} \]

8 Tensors

In linear algebra, the tensor product of vector spaces \(V,W\) is a vector space \[ V\otimes W \] consisting of elements \(v\otimes w\) with the identifications \[ \begin{aligned} \lambda(v\otimes w) &= (\lambda v)\otimes w = v\otimes(\lambda w) \quad &&\text{for }\lambda\in\mathbb R \\[4pt] (v_1+v_2)\otimes w &= v_1\otimes w+v_2\otimes w \\[2pt] v\otimes(w_1+w_2) &= v\otimes w_1+v\otimes w_2 \end{aligned} \]

Note that \[ \dim(V\otimes W)=\dim V\cdot\dim W \]

Example \[ V^*\otimes V^*=\{\text{bilinear forms }V\times V\to\mathbb R\} \]

Given vector bundles \(E,F\) over \(M\), we can take the tensor products of fibers to define \[ E\otimes F:=\bigcup_{p\in M}\big(E_p\otimes F_p\big) \]

Definition.
A tensor on \(M\) is a section of the bundle \[ T^k_{\ \ell}M:=\underbrace{T^*M\otimes\cdots\otimes T^*M}_{k\text{ copies}} \otimes \underbrace{TM\otimes\cdots\otimes TM}_{\ell\text{ copies}} \]

Example: \[ g\ \text{is a symmetric }(2,0)\text{ tensor} \]
