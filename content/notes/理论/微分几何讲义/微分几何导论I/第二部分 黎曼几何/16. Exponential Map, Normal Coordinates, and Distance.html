16. Exponential Map, Normal Coordinates, and Distance

2025-10-22

1. Exponential map

1.1 Definition

Definition 1.1Exponential map

Define the exponential map \[ \exp : U \subset TM \longrightarrow M \] by \[ v \in T_pM \longmapsto \exp(v) = \gamma_v(1) \] where \(\gamma_v\) is the geodesic with \[ \gamma_v(0) = p, \qquad \dot\gamma_v(0) = v \] and \(v \in U\) if \(\gamma_v\) exists at \(t=1\).

(Geometrically, \(\exp(v)\) is the point reached at time \(1\) by the geodesic starting from \(p\) with initial velocity \(v\).)

1.2 Re-scaling lemma for geodesics

Lemma 1.2Re-scaling a constant–speed geodesic

If \(\gamma\) is a geodesic of constant speed \[ r = \|\dot\gamma\| \] then the curve \[ \eta(t) := \gamma(c t) \] is a geodesic of speed \(c r\).

Proof sketch. The chain rule gives \[ \|\dot \eta\| = c\|\dot\gamma\| \] and one checks \[ \nabla_{\dot \eta}\dot \eta = c^2 \nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma \] so \({} \nabla_{\dot \eta}\dot \eta = 0 {}\) whenever \(\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = 0\).

1.3 Corollary: geodesics as exponentials

From the lemma,

Remark 1.3Corollary

For a geodesic \(\gamma_v\) with initial data \((\gamma_v(0)=p,\dot\gamma_v(0)=v)\), \[ \gamma_v(t) = \exp(t v) \] as long as both sides are defined.

1.4 Openness and smoothness of the domain

Proposition 1.4Openness and smoothness of \(\exp\)

\(U\) is an open subset of the \(0\)–section in \(TM\), and \(\exp\) is a smooth map.

Idea of proof (ODE regularity). Consider an ODE system \[ \begin{aligned} \dot u^j &= f^j(t,u) \quad\text{where } f^j \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{m+1}),\ j=1,\dots,m \\[4pt] u^j(0) &= a^j, \quad j=1,\dots,m \end{aligned} \] which encodes the geodesic equation in local coordinates. By the standard theory of smooth ODEs, there is a unique solution \(u(t;a)\), and

\[ \underbrace{u(t;a)}_{\text{solution}} \quad\text{is smooth as a function of}\quad \underbrace{(t,a) \in \mathbb{R}^{m+1}}_{\text{initial data and time}} \]

This gives smooth dependence on initial data and shows that the set of initial velocities \(v\) for which the solution exists at \(t=1\) is open, i.e. \(U\) is open, and that \(\exp\) is smooth.

2. Differential of the exponential map

Fix \(p\in M\) and write \(\exp_p : T_pM \to M\).

Since \[ \exp_p : T_pM \longrightarrow M \] its differential at \(X\in T_pM\) is a linear map \[ d(\exp_p)_X : T_X(T_pM) \longrightarrow T_{\exp_p(X)}M \]

Since \(T_pM\) is a vector space, we have a canonical identification \[ T_X(T_pM) \cong T_pM \] for each \(X\in T_pM\).

This identification means that for \(V\in T_pM\) we can define \[ d(\exp_p)_X V := \left.\frac{d}{dt}\exp_p(X + tV)\right|_{t=0} \]

2.1 Specialization at the origin

In particular, \[ \begin{aligned} d(\exp_p)_0 V &= \left.\frac{d}{dt}\exp_p(tV)\right|_{t=0} \\[4pt] &= \underbrace{\dot\gamma_V(0)}_{\text{initial velocity of the geodesic with }\dot\gamma_V(0)=V} \\[4pt] &= V \end{aligned} \]

Lemma 2.1Differential at the origin

\[ d(\exp_p)_0 = I \] i.e. \(d(\exp_p)_0\) is the identity map on \(T_pM\).

2.2 Inverse function theorem and local diffeomorphism

Theorem 2.2Inverse function theorem (Euclidean version)

For \(f : U_1 \to V_1\) between open subsets of \(\mathbb{R}^n\), if \(d f(x) : \mathbb{R}^n \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^n\) is invertible for some \(x\in U_1\), then there exist neighborhoods \(U_2\subset U_1\) of \(x\) and \(V_2\subset V_1\) of \(f(x)\) such that \[ f : U_2 \longrightarrow V_2 \] is a diffeomorphism.

Applying this to \(\exp_p\) in normal coordinates and using \(d(\exp_p)_0 = I\):

Proposition 2.3Local diffeomorphism property of \(\exp_p\)

For each \(p\in M\) there exist neighborhoods \(U \subset T_pM\) and \(V \subset M\) such that \[ \exp_p : U \longrightarrow V \] is a diffeomorphism.

2.3 Example: the unit sphere

Let \(S^2\) be the unit sphere in \(\mathbb{R}^3\). For \(p\in S^2\), the map \(\exp_p\) is a diffeomorphism as long as we do not hit the antipode \(-p\). Concretely, \[ \exp_p : \{\, X \mid |X| < \pi \,\} \longrightarrow S^2 \setminus \{-p\} \] ## 3. Geodesic normal coordinates and uniformly normal neighborhoods

3.1 Geodesic normal coordinates

Choose an orthonormal basis \(\{E_j\}\) for \(T_pM\).

Definition 3.1Geodesic normal coordinates at \(p\)

Define \[ \Psi(x^1,\dots,x^n) = \exp_p(x^j E_j) \]

The image of \[ \{\, |x| < r \,\} \] under \(\Psi\) is called a geodesic ball \[ B_r(p) \subset M \]

The radial curves in \(B_r(p)\) (curves of the form \(t\mapsto \Psi(tx)\)) are geodesics by the definition of \(\exp_p\).

Proposition 3.2Metric in geodesic normal coordinates

In geodesic normal coordinates at \(p\), \[ g_{ij}\big|_p = \delta_{ij}, \qquad \partial_k g_{ij}\big|_p = 0 \] (trivial exercise)

3.2 Uniformly normal neighborhoods

Definition 3.3Uniformly normal neighborhood

A neighborhood \(W \subset M\) is uniformly normal if there exists \(S \ge 0\) so that each point \(p\in W\) has a geodesic ball of radius at least \(S\).

Heuristically: every point has a geodesic ball of a uniform lower bound on the radius.

The pictures compare a uniformly normal region (like a cylinder) and a non-uniform one (like a cone narrowing to a tip).

Lemma 3.4Existence of uniformly normal neighborhoods

Any point \(p\in M\) admits a uniformly normal neighborhood.

Illustration: there is a small ball \(B_\varepsilon(p)\) contained in a larger uniformly normal neighborhood.

The main idea is to compare, in normal coordinates, the Euclidean norm to the \(g\)–norm.

4. Geodesics and distance

4.1 Regular curves and length

Definition 4.1Regular curve

A curve \[ \gamma : I \longrightarrow M \] is regular if \[ \dot\gamma \neq 0 \quad \text{for all } t \] (which implies it admits a unit speed reparametrization).

For regular curves \(\gamma : [a,b] \to M\), define the length functional \[ L(\gamma) := \int_a^b \|\dot\gamma\|\,dt \]

This is easily extended to piecewise regular curves, i.e. continuous curves that are a finite union of regular segments.
