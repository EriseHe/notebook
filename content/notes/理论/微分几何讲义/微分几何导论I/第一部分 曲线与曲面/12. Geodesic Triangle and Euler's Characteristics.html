12. Geodesic Triangle and Euler's Characteristics

2025-10-06

1 Geodesic triangles

Consider a triangle \(T\) with all sides geodesic.

We will assume \(T\) fits in a geodesic polar patch centered at \(A\).

Parametrize the non-radial side by a unit–speed geodesic \[ \gamma(t) = (r(t),\theta(t)) \]

With \[ ds^{2} = dr^{2} + \phi^{2}\,d\theta^{2}, \] the geodesic equations become \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}(\dot r) &= \phi\,(\partial_{r}\phi)\,\dot\theta^{2}\\[4pt] \frac{d}{dt}(\phi^{2}\dot\theta) &= \phi\,(\partial_{\theta}\phi)\,\dot\theta^{2} \end{aligned} \]

Define an orthonormal frame \[ e_{1} = \partial_{r},\qquad e_{2} = \frac{1}{\phi}\,\partial_{\theta}. \]

Let \(\psi\) be the angle from \(e_{1}\) to \(\dot\gamma\) (\(>0\) since \(\gamma\) is not radial). Since \(\dot\gamma\) is a unit vector, \[ \dot\gamma = \cos\psi\,e_{1} + \sin\psi\,e_{2} \] Compare this to the chain rule \[ \dot\gamma = \dot r\,\partial_{r} + \dot\theta\,\partial_{\theta}, \] so \[ \dot r = \cos\psi\qquad \dot\theta = \frac{\sin\psi}{\phi} \] Then \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}(\cos\psi) &= \ddot r \\[4pt] -\sin\psi\,\dot\psi &= \underbrace{\phi\,\dot\theta^{2}\,\partial_{r}\phi}_{\text{geodesic eq.}} \\[4pt] &= \dot\theta\,(\phi\dot\theta)\,\partial_{r}\phi = \dot\theta\,\sin\psi\,\partial_{r}\phi, \end{aligned} \] so (since \(\sin\psi\neq 0\) along the non-radial side) \[ \dot\psi = -\,\dot\theta\,\partial_{r}\phi. \]

Conclusion: along \(\gamma\) we have \[ \boxed{\displaystyle \frac{d\psi}{d\theta} = -\,\partial_{r}\phi} \]

2 Gauss–Bonnet Theorem (triangle version)

For a geodesic triangle \(T\) within a geodesic coordinate patch, with interior angles \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) at the vertices, \[ \boxed{\displaystyle \int_{T} K\,dA = (\alpha + \beta + \gamma) - \pi.} \]

2.1 Proof (in a geodesic polar patch)

Write \(T\) in geodesic polar coordinates \((r,\theta)\) centered at one vertex as \[ 0 \le \theta \le \alpha,\qquad 0 \le r \le r(\theta), \] where \(r(\theta)\) describes the non-radial side \(\gamma\). In these coordinates, \[ K = -\,\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\phi}{\phi},\qquad dA = \phi\,dr\,d\theta \] so \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{T} K\,dA &= \int_{0}^{\alpha}\!\!\int_{0}^{r(\theta)} \underbrace{\left(-\frac{\partial_{r}^{2}\phi}{\phi}\right)}_{K}\, \underbrace{\phi\,dr\,d\theta}_{dA} \\[4pt] &= \int_{0}^{\alpha}\!\!\int_{0}^{r(\theta)} -\partial_{r}^{2}\phi\,dr\,d\theta \\[4pt] &= \int_{0}^{\alpha} \big[-\partial_{r}\phi\big]_{r=0}^{r=r(\theta)}\,d\theta \\[4pt] &= \int_{0}^{\alpha} \big(1 - \partial_{r}\phi(r(\theta),\theta)\big)\,d\theta \end{aligned} \]

Along the side \(\gamma\) we have \[ \frac{d\psi}{d\theta} = -\,\partial_{r}\phi \] so \[ 1 - \partial_{r}\phi = 1 + \frac{d\psi}{d\theta} \] and therefore \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{T} K\,dA &= \int_{0}^{\alpha}\left(1 + \frac{d\psi}{d\theta}\right)\!d\theta \\[4pt] &= \alpha + \psi(\alpha) - \psi(0) \end{aligned} \]

A little angle–chasing at the two non-center vertices shows \[ \psi(\alpha) - \psi(0) = \beta + \gamma - \pi \] so \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{T} K\,dA &= \alpha + \psi(\alpha) - \psi(0) \\[4pt] &= \alpha + \beta + \gamma - \pi \end{aligned} \]

3 Global version

Let \(S\) be a compact surface without boundary.

By covering \(S\) with geodesic patches, we can subdivide \(S\) into geodesic triangles: \[ S = \bigcup_j T_j \] On each triangle, \[ \int_{T_j} K\,dA = (\alpha_j + \beta_j + \gamma_j) - \pi \]

For the triangulation \(\{T_j\}\) let \[ v = \#\text{ vertices},\quad e = \#\text{ edges},\quad f = \#\text{ faces}. \]

No boundary implies \[ 3f = 2e \] (each of the \(f\) triangles has \(3\) edges, and each edge is shared by \(2\) triangles).

Summing over faces gives \[ \begin{aligned} \int_{S} K\,dA &= \sum_{j=1}^{f}(\alpha_j + \beta_j + \gamma_j - \pi) \\[4pt] &= \underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{f}(\alpha_j + \beta_j + \gamma_j)}_{\text{sum of all angles at vertices}} - \pi f \\[4pt] &= 2\pi v - \pi f \\[4pt] &= 2\pi\,(v - e + f) = 2\pi\,\underbrace{(v - e + f)}_{\chi(S)}. \end{aligned} \]

Thus the integral of curvature depends only on the Euler characteristic \(\chi(S)\), which is independent of the chosen triangulation.

3.1 Gauss–Bonnet Theorem (compact surface)

For a compact surface \(S\), \[ \boxed{\displaystyle \int_{S} K\,dA = 2\pi\,\chi(S).} \]

For a closed, orientable surface of genus \(g\) (number of “holes”), \[ \chi(S) = 2 - 2g. \]

3.2 Full version (with boundary)

On an oriented surface \(S\), let \(R\) be a compact region with piecewise smooth boundary \(\partial R\). Then \[ \boxed{\displaystyle \int_{R} K\,dA \;+\; \int_{\partial R} k_{g}\,ds \;=\; 2\pi\,\chi(R) \;-\; \sum (\text{exterior angles}).} \]

4 Smooth manifolds

A smooth manifold \(M\) is a second countable Hausdorff space equipped with a smooth atlas.

  • Second countable:
    The topology of \(M\) is generated by a countable collection of open sets.

  • Hausdorff:
    Any two points \(p,q\in M\) are contained in disjoint open sets.

  • Smooth atlas:
    \(M\) is covered by open sets \(U_j\) with homeomorphisms \[ \phi_j : V_j \subset \mathbb{R}^{n} \longrightarrow U_j, \] such that the transition maps \[ \phi_i^{-1}\circ \phi_j : \phi_j^{-1}(U_i\cap U_j) \to \phi_i^{-1}(U_i\cap U_j) \] are smooth on overlaps.

(Whitney Embedding Theorem: without loss of generality, we can assume \(M \subset \mathbb{R}^{2n}\))
