4.3 The Electric Displacement

4.3.1 Gauss’s Law in the Presence of Dielectrics

Recall: effect of polarization = accumulations of (bound) charges. \[ \begin{align} \rho_{b} & =-\nabla \cdot \mathbf{P} \quad \text{ within the dielectric}\\ \sigma_{b} & =\mathbf{P}\cdot \hat{n} \qquad\text{ on the surface} \end{align} \]

The field attributables to

  • bound charge
  • everything else, or so-called “free charge”, \(\rho_{f}\)

Therefore, the Gauss’s law reads: \[\begin{align} ε_0∇·\underbrace{ E }_{ \text{total field} } & =ρ =\underbrace{ ρ_b }_{ −∇·P }+\underbrace{ ρ_f }_{ \text{free charge} } \\ \end{align} \] and then combine the gardient, \[\begin{align} ε_{0}∇\cdot E + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{ P} & = \rho_{f} \\ \nabla \cdot(\underbrace{ \epsilon_{0}\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{P} }_{ \mathbf{D} }) & =\rho_{f} \end{align}\] where \(\mathbf{D}\) is known as electric displacement.

TheoremGauss’s Law for Dielectrics

Gauss’s law is formulated as \[\begin{align} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{D} & = \nabla \cdot\rho_{f}\; \qquad (\text{Eq. 4.22}) \\\text{or} \qquad \int \mathbf{D}\cdot d\mathbf{a} & =Q_{f\text{enc}}\;\;\qquad (\text{Eq. 4.23}) \end{align} \] where \(\mathbf{D} = \epsilon_{0}\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{P}\) and \(Q_{f\text{enc}}\) = total free charge enclosed.

this is useful for dialectics because only free charges (the stuff we control) are referenced. In practice, we can just get \(\mathbf{D}\) even we only know \(\rho_{f}\).
